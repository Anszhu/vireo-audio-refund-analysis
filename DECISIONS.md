# Analytical decisions

Each entry: **Decision · Evidence · Reason · Impact · Limitation**. All numbers are generated by `python -m src.reports` from the supplied data.

## 1. Duplicate migration / re-import records
* **Decision:** a ticket_id present under both source systems is a re-import; keep one row (the helpdesk copy), drop the legacy copy. Dropped rows are saved to `data/processed/dropped_reimport_rows.csv`.
* **Evidence:** 638 ticket_ids appear twice, always once as `helpdesk` and once as `legacy_fd`, identical in every field except the refund amount (×100). Policy §9 says legacy tickets were re-imported during reconciliation.
* **Reason:** counting both would double-count refunds; the helpdesk copy is already in rupees.
* **Impact:** removes 125 duplicate refund rows (₹3.61 crore at raw, mixed-unit value) - about 16% of the raw total.
* **Limitation:** only exact ticket_id matches were searched; near-duplicates with different IDs were not.

## 2. Legacy monetary values
* **Decision:** divide `legacy_fd` refund amounts by 100 (paise → rupees); helpdesk amounts unchanged. Raw amount kept in `refund_amount_raw`.
* **Evidence:** exactly 100× on 125/125 re-imported pairs; median refund/retail-price ratio 90 raw vs 0.90 for helpdesk, and 0.90 for the 650 legacy-only refund rows after ÷100; 1,501/1,503 rupee amounts typed in agent notes agree after ÷100.
* **Reason:** the divisor is derived from the data; the supplied email thread says legacy money is stored "the way Freshdesk stored money".
* **Impact:** the main driver of the crore-vs-lakh gap (₹18.73 crore removed after de-duplication).
* **Limitation:** evidence strongly supports the paise-scale interpretation: exact ×100 reconciliation on all 125 re-imported refund pairs plus retail-price and agent-note support. The client did not independently confirm the unit during the assignment window, so it remains a documented analytical assumption rather than a client-confirmed fact.

## 3. Choosing the canonical ticket
* **Decision:** one row per ticket_id; helpdesk copy preferred, otherwise the only row.
* **Evidence:** all 638 dropped rows are legacy copies; canonical ticket_id is unique (11,600 rows = 12,238 raw − 638).
* **Reason:** the two copies differ only by unit, so preferring the rupee copy avoids a conversion where none is needed.
* **Impact:** 11,600 canonical tickets, approximately 644 tickets/month in the supplied 18-month dataset.
* **Limitation:** if the copies had differed in other fields this rule would hide it; they do not in this extract.

## 4. Monthly attribution
* **Decision:** month and quarter use ticket `created_at` (IST).
* **Evidence:** the refund date is not in the data; legacy `resolved_at` was rebuilt from a UTC event log (policy §9), so a resolution-based month could shift near month-end.
* **Reason:** `created_at` is the only timestamp present and consistently sourced on every row.
* **Impact:** monthly and quarterly figures are stable and reproducible; totals are unaffected.
* **Limitation:** a refund raised days after creation is attributed to the creation month.

## 5. Why raw refund totals are not used blindly
* **Decision:** every board-pack number comes from the canonical set, with a bridge raw → less duplicates → less unit scaling → canonical (`refund_reconciliation.csv`).
* **Evidence:** raw ₹23.01 crore vs canonical ₹67.10 lakh; the bridge closes to the rupee (automated check).
* **Reason:** Finance asked that the total reconcile; an unexplained figure cannot be defended in a board pack.
* **Impact:** ₹11.18 lakh a quarter, consistent with the helpdesk's ≈₹11 lakh.
* **Limitation:** how the helpdesk computed its ₹11 lakh is undocumented; consistency is shown, not identity.

## 6. Agents are not ranked globally by refund value
* **Decision:** agents are compared only with their own team, on refund rate per resolved/closed ticket (z-score against team rate), never on total value.
* **Evidence:** team refund rates differ by design (12% to 53%); largest agent deviation from own team is 1.8 standard errors, 0 agents beyond ±3.
* **Reason:** a global ranking would mostly rank teams, and Returns Desk / Billing handle refunds by design (policy §6).
* **Impact:** no individual is singled out; the agent tables remain available by month and reason.
* **Limitation:** binomial approximation; no adjustment for product, channel or case mix; refunds are attributed to the resolving agent only.

## 7. Tier 2 is not compared with Tier 1 on volume
* **Decision:** Escalations & Warranty (Tier 2) is shown in its own row and never ranked against Tier 1 on volume.
* **Evidence:** policy §6 says Tier 2 work is multi-touch and measured in days, and Tier 2 agents are not to be compared with Tier 1 on volume.
* **Reason:** follows the client's own policy.
* **Impact:** Tier 2 has 82 refund tickets, 4.5% of value.
* **Limitation:** resolution time in days was not analysed (outside the ask).

## 8. Why Returns Desk context matters
* **Decision:** report Returns Desk as a refund-handling team and test the claim that it does "most" refunds.
* **Evidence:** policy §6 says it processes the large majority of refunds by design; in the data it resolves 26% of refund tickets (25% of value) and Billing 25%.
* **Reason:** its refund rate (53%) is high by design and would look anomalous without context; the "large majority" claim does not hold on `agent_id`.
* **Impact:** team differences are not read as agent behaviour.
* **Limitation:** team = the resolving agent's roster team; `assigned_team` (first routed) can differ.

## 9. GW-OTHER is a review signal, not proof of wrongdoing
* **Decision:** report GW-OTHER as **requires review**, with the recorded code left unchanged.
* **Evidence:** 991 refunds worth ₹29.07 lakh in total (43% of canonical value); 879 (89%) exceed the ₹500 goodwill cap and those 879 refunds are worth ₹28.72 lakh; 708 of them name a specific ordinary reason in the text, only 13 describe goodwill; it is the first dropdown option according to the supplied email thread.
* **Reason:** an above-cap GW-OTHER refund is either a cap/approval issue or a mis-coded ordinary refund; the data cannot distinguish the two, and the cap applies to goodwill credits only.
* **Impact:** points reviewers to a coding-control gap without accusing anyone.
* **Limitation:** Team Lead approval is not in the data; text categories come from rules and are QA prompts.

## 10. Rules are QA assistance, not the source of record
* **Decision:** the agent's dropdown `refund_reason_code` stays the source of record; deterministic text rules only suggest a reason and detect "both remedies" statements.
* **Evidence:** hold-out (AI-labelled, not independent) exception class 17/18; known failure types are listed in the validation report; a 40-case AI secondary review agreed on 37 cases, with 0 FP / 0 FN under the existing comparison. This is supplemental evidence, not independent human validation; no person completed independent validation during the assignment window.
* **Reason:** the text is templated and typo-ridden; rules are free, reproducible and inspectable, and no evidence suggested an LLM would beat them here.
* **Impact:** ₹0 model cost per run, no external dependency, same result every run.
* **Limitation:** new phrasings will be missed; classes other than "confirmed" are prompts to look, not findings.

## 11. Refund + replacement confidence levels
* **Decision:** three levels. *Confirmed* = refund amount and `replacement_issued=Y` on the same ticket, note does not say the replacement was declined. *Likely* = flag N but the agent note says both were given. *Ambiguous* = fields contradict the note, the note only mentions a replacement, or the replacement is on another ticket of the same order. Text alone is never promoted to confirmed; customer messages are shown as context but do not classify.
* **Evidence:** 165 confirmed, 179 likely, 197 ambiguous; each exception row carries its evidence and evidence source (structured field, agent note, or cross-ticket/order evidence).
* **Reason:** an early version called same-order cross-ticket cases "likely"; they can be separate incidents, so they were downgraded. 53 of the 173 cross-ticket cases also rest on an order inferred from customer+SKU.
* **Impact:** the financial opportunity uses confirmed cases only; likely cases are shown separately as unverified upside.
* **Limitation:** "confirmed" means two system fields agree, not that a unit physically shipped (dispatch is not in the data).

## 12. No LLM dependency
* **Decision:** no LLM or paid API is called; text QA is deterministic rules. The production pipeline depends on no external model.
* **Evidence:** the notes are short, templated and typo-ridden; rules were tested on 100 AI-labelled tickets (not independent). A test fails if `src/` imports a network or LLM client.
* **Reason:** ₹0 per run, same result every run, inspectable rules, no keys to leak, and nothing showed an LLM would do better on this data.
* **Impact:** reproducible and free; a reviewer can read every rule.
* **Limitation:** new phrasings will be missed; an LLM might catch some. That was not tested and is not claimed either way.

## 13. Deliberately excluded
* **Decision:** not built - an LLM classifier; re-coding GW-OTHER; agent league tables by value; SLA, handle-time, CSAT-driver and transfer analysis; product-lot analysis; forecasting; near-duplicate search; a refund-date reconstruction; separating ₹350 SLA-breach credits (policy §3), which have no field of their own.
* **Evidence:** none of these is needed to answer "by reason code and by agent, reconciled". Lot rates rested on 30-60 tickets with no clear signal.
* **Reason:** the brief has a ~5-hour cap; correctness and reconciliation come first.
* **Impact:** a smaller tool that can be defended line by line.
* **Limitation:** the Q4 "stop arguing" hypothesis is only partly tested (refund rate flat, volume up; CSAT +0.4 not visible).
